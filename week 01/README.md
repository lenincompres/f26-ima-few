# Week 01 — The Browser as a Storytelling Space

## Question

**What can a website be?**

## We Explored

We began with the web we already know. Each of us shared a website we love, hate, visit often, or simply find curious. Together, we looked at what drew us to these sites, what they ask us to do, what delights or frustrates us, and the different ways structure, design, navigation, and interaction shape our experience.

From there, we began looking beneath the surface: the browser as a publishing and storytelling space, client/server relationships, front end and back end, HTML/CSS/JavaScript, UI/UX, and how a webpage becomes something we can see, use, and interact with.

Finally, we began making the web ourselves, using HTML to structure, arrange, link, and publish information.

## Experiment

### The Internet Needs to Know

Create a small HTML experience that convinces us that something ordinary is **extraordinary**.

It could be a restaurant, an object, a place, an animal, a person, a habit, a food, something invented—or something else entirely.

**Truth is optional. HTML is not.**

Your page should include:

- A clear declaration or headline
- Supporting text or a short story
- At least one image or media element
- At least one link to somewhere else on the web
- Enough structure and hierarchy to give the experience an order

For now, focus on what you can accomplish with **HTML alone**. Don't worry about making it visually polished yet.

If you're new to HTML, use the resources above and experiment.

If you already know HTML, challenge yourself to think about the structure itself:

- Can the order of information create a joke, surprise, or revelation?
- Can a link become part of the experience?
- What can HTML alone do?

The goal is not perfection. **Make something, try things, break things, and notice what happens.**

## Field Notes

Every week, keep a small record of your exploration.

Field Notes are not graded for correctness, completeness, or polish. They are not a summary of what happened in class or a place to prove that you understood everything. Think of them as a record of your own process of discovery: what captured your curiosity, what confused you, what surprised you, what failed, what connections you noticed, and what questions you are taking with you.

Scientists, artists, naturalists, detectives, and explorers keep field notes to document what they encounter while they are still figuring things out. Do the same here. Notes can include observations, questions, screenshots, sketches, code fragments, failed experiments, links, diagrams, or anything else that helps you notice your own learning.

You do not need to answer every question below. They are prompts to help you notice:

- What surprised or delighted you?
- What confused you?
- What did you try?
- What failed, and what did the failure reveal?
- What patterns or connections did you notice?
- What does this remind you of?
- What question are you leaving with?
- What do you notice now that you would not have noticed before?
